RESEARCH NOTES

Recognising possible cancer symptoms

Cancer can cause different symptoms, but most of these symptoms can also have many other causes. If a new or unusual change persists for several weeks or concerns you, speak with a qualified healthcare professional.

01

Breasts

A new lump or firm area, nipple changes or unusual discharge, or persistent skin changes.

02

Mouth

A persistent red or white patch, sore, bleeding, pain, or numbness.

03

Skin

A new or changing mole, a sore that doesn't heal, or a persistent unusual skin growth.

04

Neck, armpits, or groin

A new or persistent lump or swelling.

05

Bowel or bladder

Persistent changes in bowel habits, blood in stool or urine, or difficulty or pain when urinating.

06

Breathing or voice

A cough or hoarseness that doesn't go away.

07

Swallowing or digestion

Persistent difficulty swallowing, indigestion, abdominal discomfort, nausea, or appetite changes.

08

General changes

Unexplained persistent fatigue, fever or night sweats, or unexplained weight changes.

Important

These changes do not automatically mean cancer. Many are caused by infections, injuries, benign conditions, or other illnesses. Professional assessment is important when a change persists.

Source: National Cancer Institute (NCI), Symptoms of Cancer — updated May 16, 2019.
